================================================================================
TASK 8: NORMALIZATION AND KMEANS CLUSTERING (K=6) ON GLASS.ARFF IN WEKA
Dataset: glass.arff (214 instances, chemical oxide concentrations, 6 glass types)
Operations: Normalize attributes -> SimpleKMeans (k=6) -> Centroid Analysis -> Save Cluster Column
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: LOAD GLASS.ARFF IN WEKA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Launch WEKA GUI Chooser -> click "Explorer".
2. Under the "Preprocess" tab:
   - Click "Open file...".
   - Locate and select "glass.arff" (inside Weka's data directory).
   - Click "Open".
3. Check the Attributes:
   - 9 numeric features: RI (refractive index), Na, Mg, Al, Si, K, Ca, Ba, Fe.
   - 1 nominal target: Type (6 distinct glass types: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2: APPLY NORMALIZATION FILTER TO ALL ATTRIBUTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
*Why Normalize*: Chemical elements have different scales (e.g., Si is ~70%, 
while Fe is ~0.05%). Normalization scales all attributes into the uniform range [0, 1], 
preventing dominant attributes from skewing Euclidean distance calculations.

1. In the "Preprocess" tab, look at the "Filter" box (top-left).
2. Click "Choose".
3. Expand: filters -> unsupervised -> attribute -> Normalize
   (Path: weka.filters.unsupervised.attribute.Normalize).
4. (Optional) Click on the text box "Normalize" to view settings:
   - scale: 1.0
   - translation: 0.0
   - (This transforms values using formula: (x - min) / (max - min)).
   - Click "OK".
5. Click the "Apply" button next to Choose.
6. Verify: Click on any attribute (like RI or Na) in the Attributes list. 
   Notice in the right panel that Min is 0.0 and Max is 1.0.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3: RUN SIMPLEKMEANS WITH 6 CLUSTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Click on the "Cluster" tab at the top.
2. Select Clusterer:
   - Click "Choose" -> expand: clusterers -> SimpleKMeans.
3. Configure Parameters:
   - Click on the "SimpleKMeans" text box to open properties:
     * numClusters: 6 (since the domain has 6 glass types)
     * distanceFunction: weka.core.EuclideanDistance
     * seed: 10
     * maxIterations: 500
   - Click "OK".
4. Set Cluster Mode:
   - Option A: "Classes to clusters evaluation" -> select "(Nom) Type".
     (Evaluates how closely the 6 unsupervised clusters align with ground-truth glass types).
   - Option B: "Use training set" (pure unsupervised analysis).
5. Click "Start".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4: ANALYZE CLUSTER CENTROIDS FROM CLUSTEREVALUATION OUTPUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the "Clusterer output" panel on the right, locate the "Final cluster centroids" table:

Fe               0.1118             0.1200       0.0210       ...

How to Interpret the Centroids:
1. High Barium (Ba) / Aluminum (Al) Cluster:
   - Look for the cluster with an unusually high Ba centroid (~0.5 - 0.7). 
     This cleanly isolates Type 7 (Headlamps glass), which specifically uses barium.
2. High Magnesium (Mg) vs. Low Mg Clusters:
   - Building and vehicle window float glasses (Types 1 & 2) have high Magnesium (~0.75 - 0.85).
   - Tableware / containers (Types 5 & 6) have near-zero Magnesium (~0.05 - 0.15) but higher Calcium/Sodium.
3. Refractive Index (RI):
   - Clusters with high Calcium (Ca) also show elevated Refractive Index (RI) due to optical density.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5: SAVE CLUSTER ASSIGNMENTS IN A NEW ATTRIBUTE COLUMN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
There are two distinct ways to save the cluster assignments as a new attribute column:

METHOD 1: Using the "AddCluster" Filter in WEKA (Recommended - Permanent Column)
1. Go back to the "Preprocess" tab.
2. Under "Filter", click "Choose".
3. Expand: filters -> unsupervised -> attribute -> AddCluster.
4. Click on the text box "AddCluster" to configure it:
   - Click on the "clusterer" parameter.
   - Click "Choose" -> select "clusterers -> SimpleKMeans".
   - Click on the SimpleKMeans text and change "numClusters" to 6. Click "OK".
5. Click "OK" on the AddCluster property window.
6. Click the "Apply" button.
7. Verification:
   - Look at the bottom of the "Attributes" list on the left.
   - A brand new 11th attribute named "cluster" is now appended to the dataset!
8. Save the Updated Dataset:
   - Click the "Save..." button at the top-right of the Preprocess pane.
   - Enter filename: "glass_with_clusters.arff" -> click "Save".

METHOD 2: Saving via Visualizer:
1. In the "Cluster" tab, ensure the run is completed.
2. In the "Result list" (bottom-left), right-click on the "SimpleKMeans" result.
3. Select "Visualize cluster assignments".
4. In the pop-up window, click the "Save" button (bottom-right).
5. Name the file "glass_cluster_assignments.arff" and save. 
   (This file appends an instance_number and a cluster attribute: cluster0 through cluster5).
