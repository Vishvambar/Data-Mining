================================================================================
TASK 13: EXPERIENCE-BASED SALARY ANALYSIS IN TABLEAU
Dataset: HR Analytics Dataset (or Employee dataset with Experience and Salary)
Fields: Experience (or TotalWorkingYears), Salary (or MonthlyIncome)
Operations: Seniority Calculated Field -> Pie Chart -> Percentage Labels & Tooltips -> Distribution Inference
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: CONNECT TO DATASET IN TABLEAU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Open Tableau Desktop.
2. Under "Connect", click "Microsoft Excel" or "Text file" (CSV) depending on your file.
3. Select your HR dataset (e.g., HR_Analytics.csv or IBM_Attrition.csv).
4. Go to "Sheet 1".
5. Note: If your dataset uses standard IBM HR column names:
   - "TotalWorkingYears" or "YearsAtCompany" represents Experience.
   - "MonthlyIncome" represents Salary.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2: CREATE CALCULATED FIELD: "SENIORITY LEVEL"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. In the Data pane, right-click on an empty space and choose "Create Calculated Field...".
2. Configure the field:
   - Field Name: Seniority Level
   - Enter Formula:
     IF [Experience] > 5 THEN "Senior"
     ELSE "Junior"
     END

   *(Note: If using IBM HR dataset columns, replace [Experience] with [TotalWorkingYears]):
     IF [TotalWorkingYears] > 5 THEN "Senior"
     ELSE "Junior"
     END
3. Verify that the calculation editor says "The calculation is valid."
4. Click "OK".
5. "Seniority Level" will now appear under Dimensions in the Data pane.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3: CONSTRUCT THE PIE CHART
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Switch Mark Type to Pie:
   - On the **Marks** card, click the dropdown menu (currently "Automatic") and select **"Pie"**.
   - Notice new tiles appear on the Marks card: **"Color"**, **"Size"**, **"Angle"**, **"Detail"**, **"Tooltip"**.

2. Add Color Slice Dimension:
   - Drag **"Seniority Level"** from Dimensions onto the **Color** tile.
   - You will see a small pie with two colors (Senior and Junior).

3. Encode Slice Proportions by Total Salary:
   - Drag **"Salary"** (or **"MonthlyIncome"**) from Measures onto the **Angle** tile.
   - The pill becomes `SUM(Salary)`. The angle/size of each pie slice now reflects 
     the total payroll expenditure for that group.

4. Adjust Overall Size:
   - In the top toolbar, change the view dropdown from "Standard" to **"Entire View"**.
   - Click on the **Size** tile on the Marks card and drag the slider to the right 
     to enlarge the pie chart so it is clearly visible.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4: ADD PERCENTAGE LABELS TO THE PIE CHART
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Add Category Label:
   - Drag "Seniority Level" from the Data pane onto the **Label** tile.
2. Add Percentage of Total Salary:
   - Drag "Salary" (or "MonthlyIncome") from the Data pane onto the **Label** tile.
   - Currently, it shows the raw currency total (`SUM(Salary)`).
   - Convert it to Percentage:
     * Right-click the `SUM(Salary)` pill that is on the **Label** tile.
     * Go to **Quick Table Calculation** -> select **"Percent of Total"**.
     * The label on the pie slices will now display exact percentages (e.g., 78.4% and 21.6%).
3. Format the Percentage:
   - Right-click that same pill on the Label tile -> "Format...".
   - In the Format pane on the left, under Numbers, choose "Percentage" -> 1 decimal place.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5: CONFIGURE TOOLTIPS TO DISPLAY AVERAGE SALARY PER GROUP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Add Average Salary to Marks:
   - Drag "Salary" (or "MonthlyIncome") from the Data pane onto the **Tooltip** tile on the Marks card.
   - It will initially show as `SUM(Salary)`.
   - Change it to Average:
     * Right-click that `SUM(Salary)` pill on Tooltip.
     * Select "Measure (Sum)" -> click **"Average"**.
     * The pill changes to `AVG(Salary)`.
2. Format the Average Salary as Currency:
   - Right-click the `AVG(Salary)` pill on Tooltip -> select "Format...".
   - Under Numbers, select "Currency (Custom)" -> Prefix: "$" or "₹", 0 decimal places.
3. Customize the Tooltip Text:
   - Click directly on the **Tooltip** tile on the Marks card to open the text editor.
   - Format the text cleanly as follows:
     ------------------------------------------------------------------
     Seniority Group: <Seniority Level>
     Total Payroll Share: <% of Total SUM(Salary)>
     Total Salary Paid: <SUM(Salary)>
     Average Salary per Employee: <AVG(Salary)>
     ------------------------------------------------------------------
   - Click "OK".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 6: INFERENCES ON SALARY DISTRIBUTION (SENIOR VS. JUNIOR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Key Insights from the Visualizations:

1. Disproportionate Payroll Concentration:
   - Senior employees (> 5 years experience) absorb approximately 75% to 82% of the company's 
     total payroll budget, while Junior employees account for only 18% to 25%.

2. Significant Average Pay Gap:
   - The Average Salary for Senior employees is typically 2.5x to 3.5x higher than that of Juniors.
   - Cause: Experience-based pay progression, compound annual merit increases, and higher 
     managerial/specialist job grades held by seniors.

3. Headcount vs. Cost Leverage:
   - Even in organizations where junior staff represent 40-50% of the headcount, their 
     collective cost footprint remains modest.

4. Strategic HR Implications:
   - Senior attrition carries severe financial and institutional disruption: replacing a 
     senior employee incurs high recruiting and onboarding costs.
   - Junior compensation must be benchmarked competitively to ensure retention through the 
     formative 0-5 year development pipeline.
