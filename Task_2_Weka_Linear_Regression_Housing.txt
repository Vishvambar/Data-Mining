================================================================================
TASK 2: LINEAR REGRESSION TO PREDICT PRICE USING HOUSING.ARFF IN WEKA
Dataset: housing.arff (Standard Boston Housing Dataset)
Target Variable: Price (numeric / continuous, often labeled 'class' or 'Price' / 'MEDV')
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: LOCATE AND LOAD THE DATASET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Locate housing.arff:
   - Weka includes standard benchmark datasets by default.
   - Typically located in:
     * Windows: C:\Program Files\Weka-3-x-x\data\housing.arff
     * Linux: /usr/share/weka/data/housing.arff (or inside weka.jar / datasets folder).
   - If downloading manually, it corresponds to the standard Boston Housing dataset.

2. Launch Weka:
   - Open the "WEKA GUI Chooser".
   - Click the "Explorer" button (top left).
   - You will start on the "Preprocess" tab.

3. Load the File:
   - Click "Open file..." at the top-right of the Preprocess pane.
   - Navigate to the folder containing housing.arff.
   - Select "housing.arff" and click "Open".

4. Verify Data in Preprocess Tab:
   - Under the "Current relation" section, verify:
     * Relation: housing
     * Instances: 506
     * Attributes: 14 (CRIM, ZN, INDUS, CHAS, NOX, RM, AGE, DIS, RAD, TAX, PTRATIO, B, LSTAT, and the target price attribute, usually 'class' or 'MEDV').
   - Check the target attribute (Price/class):
     * Click on the target attribute in the Attributes list.
     * In the "Selected attribute" box on the right, verify that Type is "Numeric".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2: CONFIGURE AND RUN LINEAR REGRESSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Switch to the "Classify" tab at the top of the Weka window (the 2nd tab).
2. Choose the Classifier:
   - Click the "Choose" button under the "Classifier" header.
   - Expand the tree:
     classifiers -> functions -> LinearRegression
     (Full class: weka.classifiers.functions.LinearRegression)
3. Set Test Options:
   - Select the "Cross-validation" radio button.
   - Set "Folds:" to 10 (standard 10-fold cross-validation for robust evaluation).
   - Alternatively, if testing on training set is desired, select "Use training set".
4. Set Target Class:
   - Look at the dropdown directly under "Test options".
   - Ensure the continuous numeric attribute representing price is selected:
     e.g., "(Num) class" or "(Num) Price".
5. Execute:
   - Click the "Start" button on the right side.
   - Wait for the bird icon at the bottom-right to stop animating.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3: LOCATE AND REPORT EVALUATION METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Look at the "Classifier output" window on the right side under the section titled:
"=== Cross-validation ===" (or "=== Summary ===")

Locate and report the following required values:

1. Correlation Coefficient:
   - Line: "Correlation coefficient                  0.85xx" (typically between 0.84 and 0.86)
   - Meaning: Measures the strength and direction of the linear relationship between
     actual house prices and predicted house prices.
     * Value close to +1.0 indicates a very strong positive linear relationship.

2. Mean Absolute Error (MAE):
   - Line: "Mean absolute error                     3.2xxx" (typically around 3.2 to 3.4)
   - Meaning: The average magnitude of the errors between predictions and actual prices,
     measured in the original units of price ($1,000s in the Boston dataset). An MAE
     of 3.27 means the model's predictions deviate by an average of ~$3,270.

3. Other Supplementary Metrics (Good to mention):
   - Root mean squared error (RMSE): Typically around 4.8 to 4.9.
   - Relative absolute error (RAE): Typically ~48%.
   - Total Number of Instances: 506.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4: INTERPRET THE MODEL OUTPUT (REGRESSION EQUATION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scroll up in the Classifier output window to:
"=== Classifier model (full training set) ==="

Linear Regression builds an equation of the form:
Price = w0 + w1*Attribute1 + w2*Attribute2 + ...

Example Weka output equation:
class =
     -0.108  * CRIM +
      0.0464 * ZN +
      0.0206 * INDUS +
      2.6867 * CHAS = 1 +
     -17.7666 * NOX +
      3.8099 * RM +
      0.0007 * AGE +
     -1.4756 * DIS +
      0.306  * RAD +
     -0.0123 * TAX +
     -0.9527 * PTRATIO +
      0.0093 * B +
     -0.5248 * LSTAT +
     36.4595

Interpretation of Key Attributes:
1. Positive Impact:
   - RM (average number of rooms per dwelling): Coefficient ~ +3.81.
     * For every additional room in a house, the predicted price increases by ~$3,810.
   - CHAS (Charles River dummy variable): Coefficient ~ +2.69.
     * Houses tract bounding the Charles River command an average premium of ~$2,690.
   - ZN (proportion of residential land zoned for lots over 25,000 sq.ft.): Positive coefficient (+0.046).

2. Negative Impact:
   - NOX (nitric oxides concentration in ppm): Large negative coefficient (~ -17.77).
     * Severe air pollution strongly decreases residential property values.
   - LSTAT (% lower status of the population): Coefficient ~ -0.52.
     * Higher proportions of lower socioeconomic status correlate with lower market prices.
   - PTRATIO (pupil-teacher ratio by town): Coefficient ~ -0.95.
     * Worse school ratios (more students per teacher) negatively impact home prices.
   - DIS (weighted distances to five Boston employment centres): Negative coefficient (~ -1.48).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5: COMMENT ON THE MODEL'S PERFORMANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write the following summary analysis:
1. Predictive Capability:
   - With a correlation coefficient of ~0.85, the model captures approximately 72% 
     of the variance (R-squared ~ 0.85^2 ≈ 0.72) in housing prices.
2. Error Magnitude:
   - An MAE of ~3.3 on median house values ranging from $5,000 to $50,000 shows reasonable 
     accuracy for a basic baseline linear model.
3. Limitations of Linear Regression:
   - Several real-world relationships in housing (e.g., pollution vs. price, distance vs. price) 
     are non-linear and may involve interactions that a plain linear model cannot fully capture.
   - Extreme values/outliers (such as capped $50k homes in Boston housing) increase the RMSE.
